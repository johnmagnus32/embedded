# screen

## Summary
I didn't change any repo files; the only tools run were pcbnew (read-only) and scripts in /tmp/gb2-window/, which also holds the spec page renders and the two downloaded datasheets. Coordinates below are board coordinates (KiCad, +Y down). For the enclosure frame, negate y; z = 0 is the board's bottom face.

**Screen position.** The 2090's PCB is centred on the device centreline (module centre (95.25, 71.0)), but its pixels are 2.64 mm left of centre. The active area is 57.6 x 43.2 mm at x 63.81..121.41, y 49.40..92.60, centre (92.61, 71.0), which is (92.61, -71.0) in the enclosure frame. The window has to follow the pixels, not the device centre. Making it look centred would need a board change (draw_mech CENTER_ON = "active-area").

**Heights.** Module back z 12.60 (8.50 ±0.15 socket + about 2.5 header body). Module front z 14.17. The glass top is z 18.27 at the left (JP1) end, which is the 16.67 above the board top that draw_mech uses. It is about 18.47 at the right (FPC) end, because the 0.3 mm FPC lies under the untaped end. Worst case the highest point is about 18.9.

**FPC bend.** The FPC folds at the panel's right edge and bulges up to 1.0 mm beyond it (x 130.01..131.01). Nothing may press there.

**Back-side parts.** They hang down to z 9.95 on the original board revision, or 10.6 on rev D (the EYESPI connector is 2.0 mm tall per its datasheet). Both are over the battery, and `enclosure.py` puts the cell top at z 10.5, so the cell collides with them.

**Mounting.** Four 3.0 mm holes, so M2.5 only, at (57.15/133.35, 42.425/99.575). Use M2.5 x 11 F-F standoffs, round and no more than 5 mm across. A 5 mm hex can leave only 0.04 mm to the socket bodies.

**Touch.** It is unused: JP1 pins 9-11 are not connected. So a separate lens with an air gap is fine, and a bezel touching the glass doesn't matter for touch. It does matter for the LCD: pressing causes TN ripple, and the driver chip and the FPC fold sit under the right end of the glass.

**Viewing direction.** The TN panel's best direction (6 o'clock) points to the device's right. Tilting the device up and down uses the symmetric ±50° axis, so there's no reason to angle the screen.

**Recommendations:**
- **Window:** 60.6 x 46.2 mm, corner radius 1.5, centred on the active area (1.5 mm beyond it on each side). The bezel then covers the glass border by 1.30 mm left, 1.65 mm top and bottom, and 6.85 mm right.
- **Lens:** 1.5 mm clear cast acrylic, laser-cut and fixed with adhesive transfer tape. Not 3D-printed, and no superglue (it fogs acrylic).
- **Gap:** put the lowest lens or shell surface over the glass at z 19.1, a 0.6-0.8 mm gap. Re-set it to the measured highest glass point + 0.5 once the real module is on its standoffs.
- **Pocket, option A (simplest):** a 65.6 x 51.2 clear lens in a 66.0 x 51.6 x ~1.73 mm pocket on a 0.8-1.0 mm printed ledge. Front face z 21.6, total depth 25.2 mm.
- **Pocket, option C' (slimmest, closest to the GBA):** a 77.5 x 55.5 lens with a black mask painted on its back, sitting directly over the glass and supported outside it. Front face z 20.7, total depth 24.3 mm, against the GBA's 24.5.

Before cutting the lens, measure the real module's glass height, its active-area position, and which board revision it is.

## Risks
- The glass-top height is the least certain number. The header body is estimated, the panel is ±0.2, and the right (FPC) end sits about 0.2 higher. In the worst case the glass reaches the planned z 19.1 surface. Measure the real stack after fitting the standoffs and make the glass height a parameter.
- The pixels' horizontal position is uncertain by about 0.3 mm (spec vs Adafruit's Eagle file), plus how the panel was taped onto the module. Measure the lit active area before cutting the lens or mask.
- The pixels are 2.64 mm left of the device centreline. Any bezel or lens symmetric about the device shows unequal side borders (5.8 vs 11.1 mm in option C'). Only a board change fixes this: draw_mech CENTER_ON='active-area' moves the module 2.64 mm toward JP2, which moves J3/J4 and the four standoff holes.
- The standoffs nearly hit the socket bodies. The HX socket is 51.3 ±0.3 mm long, so its ends are 2.925 mm from the hole centres. A 5 mm hex has corners at 2.887 mm (0.04 mm clearance, possible interference). draw_mech line 85 only checked pins and copper. Use round standoffs no wider than 5 mm, a 4.5 mm hex, or turn a flat toward the socket.
- If the standoffs don't match the header stack (8.50 ±0.15 + about 2.5), tightening them bends the module's ears or lifts the headers. Measure the gap and shim.
- Battery cross-check: enclosure.py mech() line 57 puts the cell top at z 10.5. The module's back parts over the battery reach z 9.95 on the original revision (two SOIC-20W chips, a 0.55 mm overlap) or 10.6 on rev D (EYESPI connector, 0.1 mm clearance). Find out which revision the user has (rev D has an 18-pin FPC connector on the back) and drop the cell toward the back-tray floor.
- enclosure.py models the module as one 5.7 mm box over its whole outline (z 12.6..18.3). It leaves out the back-side parts, header bodies, standoffs, screw heads and the panel tilt. As a front-shell keep-out it is over-cautious (it blocks ribs around the panel); as the battery keep-out it is unsafe.
- Printing: with the front shell printed face-down, the land under any front-loaded lens (options A and C') is an unsupported 2.5-4 mm overhang ring. It needs supports, an SLA/MJF print, or option B (lens loaded from inside behind the lip), which prints without supports.
- Cast acrylic thickness varies by about ±10%: set the pocket depth from the measured sheet. Cyanoacrylate glue fogs acrylic and the module glass; use transfer tape.
- If touch is ever enabled, any separate lens with an air gap would effectively disable it. Only option D (bare module glass behind a bezel, with no conductive or carbon-filled filament near the viewing area) would keep it working.
- Light leakage: with a light-coloured or translucent shell, backlight glow escapes at the panel edges (the LEDs are normally at the FPC end). Print the window surround in black or line it with black tape or foam.
- The GBA lens outline in mech_ref barely contains the 2090 glass (0.49 mm at its top corners), and stock GBA lenses are too short for this active area. Both need a custom lens.
